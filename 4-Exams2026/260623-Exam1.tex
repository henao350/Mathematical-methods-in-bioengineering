\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{P\arabic*.},itemsep=1em]
  \item In a bioreactor engineering experiment, the rate of biomass accumulation of a bacterial culture over the first 4 hours is modeled by the function:
  $$ r(t) = 3t^2 + 2t $$
  where $t$ is the time in hours ($h$) and $r(t)$ is the growth rate in grams per hour ($g/h$).

  The total mass of the population accumulated between $t = 0$ and $t = 2$ hours is given by the definite integral:
  $$ M = \int_{0}^{2} (3t^2 + 2t) \,\dd t $$

  Calculate the total biomass $M$ (in grams) accumulated at $t = 2$ hours.

  \medskip
  \item During a drug delivery assay, the concentration $C(t)$ of a therapeutic agent in a patient's bloodstream (measured in mg/L) as a function of time $t$ (in hours) is modeled by the rational function:
  $$ C(t) = \frac{8t^2 + 5t}{2t^2 + 3} $$

  Biomedical engineers are interested in finding the steady-state concentration, which corresponds to the limiting value of the concentration as time approaches infinity.
    Evaluate the limit:
    $$ L = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{8t^2 + 5t}{2t^2 + 3} $$

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item In a tissue engineering laboratory, electrospun polymer nanofibers are fabricated to serve as scaffolds for cell growth. The diameter of these fibers, denoted by $Y$, is known to be normally distributed with a mean of $\mu = 500$ nm and a standard deviation of $\sigma = 50$ nm (i.e., $Y \sim N(500, 50)$).

    Biomedical studies indicate that optimal cell attachment and proliferation occur only on fibers with diameters between $440$ nm and $610$ nm. Estimate the percentage of the fabricated nanofiber population that satisfies the optimal structural criteria for cell growth.

      \bigskip
    \item Let $X$ be a normally distributed random variable, $X\sim N(\mu, \sigma)$ for some real number $\mu$ and some $\sigma>0$. Define
    $$ f(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma }\exp \Bigg ( - \frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Bigg ).$$
    In this problem you can use the following (you may assume that these properties are already known):
      \begin{itemize}
      \item For any real number $a$,
      $$
          P(X<a) = \int_{-\infty}^a f(x) \,\dd x
      $$
      \item $\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^\infty z\, \exp(-z^2/2)\,\dd z =0. $
      \item $\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^\infty (x-\mu)\, f(x)\,\dd x  = \int_{-\infty}^\infty x\, f(x)\,\dd x
          - \mu \int_{-\infty}^\infty\, f(x)\,\dd x.$
      \item The quantity $\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^\infty x\, f(x)\,\dd x$ is known as \emph{the expected value of the random variable X}.
      \end{itemize}
    \medskip
    Now you are asked to answer/do the following:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item What is the value of $\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x) \,\dd x$? (An heuristic justification is enough.)
      \item Using a change of variable, prove that:
      $$
        \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (x-\mu) f(x)\,\dd x = \int_{-\infty}^\infty z\, \exp(-z^2/2)\,\dd z.
      $$
      \item Show how to combine the above properties (even if you were not able to prove them), to establish that
      \begin{center}
      \emph{The expected value of $X$ is $\mu$.}
      \end{center}
      \end{enumerate}
  \end{enumerate}

  \medskip
  \item The logistic growth of a microbial population is described by the differential equation $\frac{\dd N}{\dd t} = rN\left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right)$. Given the initial condition $N(0) = N_0$, solving this equation for the intrinsic growth rate $r$ yields the following algebraic relationship:
  $$ r = \frac{1}{t} \ln \left( \frac{K - N_0}{N_0} \right) + \frac{1}{t} \ln \left( \frac{N(t)}{K - N_t} \right) $$

  Suppose a bioengineer monitors a cell culture population and determines that the carrying capacity is $K = 30$ million cells. The population data at key checkpoints is recorded as follows:
  \begin{center}
    $N(0) = 10$ million cells \quad and \quad $N(5) = 22$ million cells.
  \end{center}

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Use the provided formula and the data points at $t = 0$ and $t = 5$ to estimate the exact value of the intrinsic growth rate $r$. \textit{(Leave your answer in terms of natural logarithms or round to four decimal places.)}
    \item Using your calculated value of $r$ and the explicit solution model, project the expected cell population size at $t = 10$ hours.
  \end{enumerate}

  \item The current $I$ (in amperes, A) flowing through an AC circuit as a function of time $t$ (in seconds) is given by:

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item What is the amplitude of the current function?
    \item What is the period of the current function?
    \item Sketch the graph of this function over a time interval covering exactly two full periods.
  \end{enumerate}

  \begin{enumerate}
   \item
   In a biomechanics study, engineers measure the relationship between the applied tensile force $x$ (in Newtons, N) and the resulting elongation $Y$ (in millimeters, mm) of a novel biocompatible hydrogel sample. The data collected from 4 trials is summari zed in the table below:

  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
    \hline
    \textbf{Force } $x_i$ (N) & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ \hline
    \textbf{Elongation } $Y_i$ (mm) & 2 & 3 & 5 & 6 \\ \hline
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \medskip
  We want to fit a linear regression line using the least-squares estimators $\hat{\beta}$ and $\hat{\alpha}$:
  $$ \hat{\beta} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})(Y_i - \bar{Y})}{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})^2}, \quad \hat{\alpha} = \bar{Y} - \hat{\beta}\bar{x} $$
  where $\bar{x} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}{n}$ and $\bar{Y} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i}{n}$.

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Compute the sample means $\bar{x}$ and $\bar{Y}$.
    \item Determine the exact values of the slope estimator $\hat{\beta}$ and the intercept estimator $\hat{\alpha}$.
    \item using the model $\hat{Y} = \hat{\alpha} + \hat{\beta}x$Predict the expected elongation $\hat{Y}$ of the hydrogel sample if a tensile force of $x = 2.5$ N is applied .
  \end{enumerate}

  \bigskip
  \item Let $S_{XY}$ be the quantity defined in the least-squares estimation formula:
  $$ S_{XY} = \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})(Y_i - \bar{Y})$$
  Mathematically prove the identity for the numerator shortcut formula:
    $$ S_{XY} = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i Y_i - n\bar{x}\bar{Y} $$
    \textit{(Hint: Expand the binomial expression inside the summation and distribute $\sum$. Recall that $\sum_{i=1}^n x_i = n\bar{x}$ and $\sum_{i=1}^n \bar{Y} = n\bar{Y}$.)}

  \end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}
