\vspace{-2em}

\item Random samples of 50 college students, 40 college faculty, and 60 bankers yielded the following data relating to the numbers of smokers in these samples:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\hline
\textbf{Group} & \textbf{Number who smoke} \\ \hline
College students & 22 \\
College faculty & 9 \\
Bankers & 25 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\medskip

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Test the hypothesis, at the 10 percent level of significance, that the same percentages of college students, college faculty, and bankers are smokers.
    \item Repeat part (a) at the 5 percent level of significance.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{mysolution}
    \textbf{Chi-Square Test for Homogeneity:}
    Let $H_0$ be the null hypothesis that the proportion of smokers is the same across all three populations, versus $H_a$ that the proportions are not all equal.

    First, we construct the full contingency table to find marginal totals ($n = 150$).

    \begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
    \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \hline
    \textbf{Group} & \textbf{Smokers} & \textbf{Non-Smokers} & \textbf{Total} \\ \hline
    \textbf{College students} & 22 (18.6667) & 28 (31.3333) & 50 \\
    \textbf{College faculty}  & 9 (14.9333)  & 31 (25.0667) & 40 \\
    \textbf{Bankers}          & 25 (22.4000) & 35 (37.6000) & 60 \\ \hline
    \textbf{Total}            & 56           & 94           & 150 \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    \nopagebreak\textit{Note: Expected counts $E_{ij} = \frac{\text{Row Total} \times \text{Column Total}}{n}$ are written in parentheses.}

    Using the test statistic formula $\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O_{ij} - E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}}$:
    \begin{align*}
    \chi^2 &= \frac{(22-18.6667)^2}{18.6667} + \frac{(28-31.3333)^2}{31.3333} + \frac{(9-14.9333)^2}{14.9333} \\
           &+ \frac{(31-25.0667)^2}{25.0667} + \frac{(25-22.4)^2}{22.4} + \frac{(35-37.6)^2}{37.6}
    \end{align*}
    \begin{align*}
    \chi^2 &= \frac{(3.3333)^2}{18.6667} + \frac{(-3.3333)^2}{31.3333} + \frac{(-5.9333)^2}{14.9333} + \frac{(5.9333)^2}{25.0667} + \frac{(2.6)^2}{22.4} + \frac{(-2.6)^2}{37.6} \\
           &= 0.5952 + 0.3546 + 2.3574 + 1.4045 + 0.3018 + 0.1798 \\
           &= 5.1933
    \end{align*}

    The degrees of freedom are:
    \[
    df = (3 - 1) \times (2 - 1) = 2
    \]

    \textbf{(a) At the 10\% significance level ($\alpha = 0.10$):}
    The critical value is $\chi^2_{0.10, 2} = 4.605$. Since our test statistic $\chi^2 = 5.1933 > 4.605$, we \textbf{reject} the null hypothesis. There is sufficient evidence at this level to conclude that the percentages of smokers are different.

    \textbf{(b) At the 5\% significance level ($\alpha = 0.05$):}
    The critical value is $\chi^2_{0.05, 2} = 5.991$. Since $\chi^2 = 5.1933 \le 5.991$, we \textbf{fail to reject} the null hypothesis.

\end{mysolution}
